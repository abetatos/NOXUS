Assumptions

Recorded so a reviewer can attack them directly.

A1 — A Tangshan-AOI NO₂ feature is a legitimate thing to score against national output

Assumed: correlating a cluster-scale feature with a national target is informative rather than a category error.

Why: that is exactly the hypothesis under test. If the AOI column is dominated by a regional common mode, it should track wide-area industrial activity better than it tracks its own cluster — and that asymmetry is the evidence.

If wrong: a reader could take a positive result as “TROPOMI over Tangshan measures Chinese steel output”, which it does not support (Tangshan is ~13% of national output and the AOI is ~1.6°×1.75°).

Mitigation: EDGE-006 and REQ-022 force the correct reading into the findings note and the paper.

Blocks implementation: no — but it is the assumption most likely to be challenged in review.

A2 — Arm A's national series are an adequate wider-scale target

Assumed: the CREA national BF Starting Rate (247 mills) is a fair national counterpart to the CREA Tangshan BF rate.

Why: same variable, same publisher, same cadence (weekly, n=383 since 2019) — so the contrast isolates spatial scale and holds everything else fixed. That is a genuinely clean experimental design.

If wrong: it is also the weakness. Shared publisher means a survey-methodology artefact would move Tangshan and national together, so Arm A cannot break the feature-vs-target ambiguity on its own.

Mitigation: stated as a limit (REQ-022); Arm B adds an independent publisher where feasible.

Blocks implementation: no.

A3 — The national-scale NO₂–industry link is real in the literature

Assumed: the pipeline-suspect branch is meaningful because a working pipeline should recover something at national scale.

Why: Morris & Zhang (2019), Li & Zheng (2023) and the 0.5–0.75 range cited throughout this project.

If wrong: those studies use national-coverage NO₂, not a single AOI. Our feature is a Tangshan window, so a low national correlation is not automatically evidence of a broken pipeline — it could simply mean the window is too small to proxy national activity.

Mitigation: the pipeline-suspect verdict is deliberately worded as “does not support A” rather than “the pipeline is broken”, and the out-of-scope note records that the decisive version of this test requires a province/nation-scale cube.

Blocks implementation: no.

A4 — Deseasonalised features remove enough shared trend

Assumed: scoring on the deseasonalised/differenced features (rather than raw levels) plus a sub-period split is sufficient protection against a spurious trend-driven contrast.

Why: the paper's Section 4 shows the raw-level −0.28 is a trend-crossing artefact that largely disappears within sub-periods; the same trap applies here.

If wrong: the unharmonised TROPOMI processor-version sequence contributes its own secular component (paper Section 2), which no deseasonalisation removes.

Mitigation: EDGE-005 requires the caveat in the findings note; the sub-period split is mandatory, not optional.

Blocks implementation: no.

A5 — 0.20 is the right pipeline-suspect threshold

Assumed: a national |r| below 0.20 is far enough under the 0.5–0.75 literature range to be informative.

Why: it sits well below the weakest published national-scale result while staying above the near-null band this project keeps measuring.

If wrong: reporting convenience only — all raw r values and CIs are published, so a reader can apply their own threshold.

Blocks implementation: no, but it must be fixed before T8 runs and never adjusted after.
