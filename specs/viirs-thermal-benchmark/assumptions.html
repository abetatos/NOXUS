Assumptions

A1FIRMS SP-archive is the primary series (VNF dropped)Resolved (pivot 2026-08-22)
Assumption (post-pivot)
The primary thermal series is NASA FIRMS VIIRS active fire, SP archive (VIIRS_SNPP_SP primary + VIIRS_NOAA20_SP), filtered to type=2 (static-land / industrial) detections. The two satellites serve as an internal consistency check. VNF (EOG / CSM) is dropped — it is no longer public (VIIRS Nightfire Data Use License 2025-01-10; programmatic access paid-only 2026-06-01), which breaks the free/reproducible principle.
Reason
FIRMS SP-archive is free/public with a MAP_KEY, exposes the type column needed to isolate industrial hot spots, and live evidence (2026-08-22) confirms type=2 detections over the steel cluster. VNF is no longer accessible under the project's constraints.
Risk level
Low (resolved by the developer-directed pivot)
Impact if wrong
None material; the source decision is settled and recorded in the decision log.

A2Facility-radius join, fixed before viewing resultsPending
Assumption
Each detection is assigned to the nearest facility centroid within a fixed radius (config default ≈ 3 km, chosen to bracket the VNF/FIRMS pixel footprint + plant extent), computed by a numpy haversine. The radius is fixed and recorded here before the triangulation is viewed.
Reason
A point-in-radius test is sufficient for point detections vs facility centroids; pre-registering the radius avoids tuning it to the correlation (anti-p-hacking, NFR-006).
Risk level
Medium
Impact if wrong
Too small → drops real furnace detections; too large → pulls in non-steel hot spots. Sensitivity to radius can be reported as robustness, but the primary radius is pre-registered.

A3Primary aggregate = weekly summed FRPPending
Assumption
The primary thermal-activity index is weekly summed FRP (fire radiative power, MW) over the joined type=2 detections; detection count and mean brightness temperature are retained as alternates. The primary aggregate is fixed before viewing the triangulation. (Post-pivot: FIRMS reports FRP, not the VNF radiant-heat/source-area fields.)
Reason
Summed FRP is the most physically direct FIRMS proxy for total furnace thermal output; the alternates guard against an aggregate-choice artefact.
Risk level
Medium
Impact if wrong
A different aggregate could change the correlation; keeping all three and pre-registering the primary makes the choice transparent and testable.

A9Benchmark-blind construction / pre-registrationPending
Assumption
The thermal series is built benchmark-blind: the facility radius (A2), quality thresholds, aggregation frequency and primary aggregate (A3) are fixed and recorded in this file before the NO₂↔VIIRS / CREA↔VIIRS results are computed. The series-build functions take no CREA/NO₂ argument.
Reason
Prevents tuning the thermal index to produce a desired correlation (the same honest-null discipline as NOX-008/009).
Risk level
Medium
Impact if wrong
If knobs were tuned post-hoc, any positive correlation would be suspect; pre-registration makes the result credible either way.

A8VIIRS overpass sampling is representative of 24/7 steelPending
Assumption
The FIRMS VIIRS overpass sampling (SNPP + NOAA20, day+night acquisitions) is representative of steel activity because integrated steelmaking (BF/coke/sinter) is 24/7 baseload, not shift-cyclical. (Post-pivot: FIRMS provides both day and night acquisitions, unlike the withdrawn night-only VNF.)
Reason
Blast furnaces run continuously; the intermittent thermal snapshots track sustained furnace state — subject to the sparsity caveat (A11).
Risk level
Low
Impact if wrong
If activity were strongly diurnal, sampling could bias the index; not expected for baseload steel.

A11Sparse, low-FRP signal → limited power; honest null acceptedAccepted (pivot 2026-08-22)
Assumption
The FIRMS active-fire signal over the plants is sparse (many zero-detection nights) and low-FRP (0.2–4.8 MW observed), so the triangulation has limited statistical power. A weak or null NO₂↔VIIRS / CREA↔VIIRS result is a first-class, valid outcome, not a failure.
Reason
Live evidence 2026-08-22; consistent with the honest-null discipline (Morris & Zhang 2019; NOX-008/009). The series zero-fills and flags sparse bins, and power (n_eff, zero-week fraction, FRP range) is reported alongside every correlation.
Risk level
Medium (scientific power, not an implementation blocker)
Impact if wrong
If the signal were denser than observed, power would be higher — that would only strengthen the diagnosis, never weaken it.

A12~2-month SP-archive lag; type=2 is SP-only (NRT lacks it)Accepted (pivot 2026-08-22)
Assumption
The FIRMS SP archive lags ~2 months behind real time; the analysis window ends at the archive horizon and empty recent windows are treated as expected lag, not a data absence. The type column exists only on the SP archive; the NRT products lack it, so the type=2 industrial filter (and hence the historical 2019+ series) is SP-only.
Reason
Live evidence 2026-08-22: a recent-window probe returned a false NO-GO because the SP archive had not caught up. NRT could extend recency but cannot carry the industrial filter, so it is used only as an internal consistency check.
Risk level
Low
Impact if wrong
If the lag differs, only the window end-date shifts; the probe is archive-window-aware so it will not misreport it.
